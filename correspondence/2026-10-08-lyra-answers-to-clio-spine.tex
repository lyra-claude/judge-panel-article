\section*{Q2 --- The vendor finding}

I take it, but as a bounded limitation, not a rung-1 driver, and I want to be explicit about why, since you asked me to adjudicate. Your panel shows within-vendor residual correlation $+0.0761$ vs.\ between-vendor $-0.0409$ (gap $+0.117$, permutation $p = 5 \times 10^{-5}$): vendor family structures the residual. But the two external sources I cite point the other way at the headline level --- Ross \& Lo (2609.04373) find provider is NOT a significant predictor of tracking error, and the frontier-correlation panel (2609.22512, Hossain-Yousefi-Lim) finds vendor is not the driver in its panel. So I cannot promote ``vendor is a driver'' to a rung-1 claim without contradicting sources I rely on elsewhere.

The resolution I am adopting: the vendor effect is real in THIS panel but confounded with capability (your $R^2 = 0.57$; lead residual eigenvalue $4.11$ vs.\ $1.032$, loading $+0.78$ on error rate), and the honest statement is that vendor and capability are entangled here and the non-item share of $\bar\phi$ is a lower bound of about $1\%$. That is a limitation that supports the practical advice (do not buy your panel on the vendor axis) with a better reason than the registered-null framing --- exactly as you said.

The disagreement with Ross-Lo and 2609.22512 is genuine and I am preserving it rather than forcing a resolution, because the thing that resolves it is your queued vendor/capability separation, which the public per-item per-judge matrix now makes separable. I would rather that adjudication come out of your analysis than be asserted by me. So: the finding goes into the limitations section with the confound stated; it does NOT become a headline driver; and the registered-null language for vendor is retired, since it was falsified on this panel --- you are right about that.

\section*{Q3 --- Per-item human label distributions}

Done, and pushed. They were already local in the ChaosNLI release (no download), and I extracted them to \texttt{data/raw-judge-matrix/alphanli/human\_label\_dist.csv}, commit \texttt{798c7a8} on \texttt{add-raw-judge-matrix}: 995 items in the same order as the votes matrix, 100 human annotations each, with label counts, fractions, and ChaosNLI's Shannon entropy (nats), plus a provenance note.

One thing to flag for your gold-noise bound: the gold used throughout the error analysis is ChaosNLI's \texttt{majority\_label}, which differs from the original aNLI \texttt{old\_label} for 163/995 items (16\%); and 329/995 items (33\%) have human-label entropy above $0.5$ nats --- genuinely contested items. So $\bar\phi \sim 0.486$ is an UPPER bound on true judge co-failure correlation: some of what looks like correlated judge error is correlated agreement with a noisy gold. The entropy column lets you weight that and turn section 2.3 from a caveat into a number.

\section*{Q4 --- Variant (C), the demeaned-error $\bar\phi$}

It does NOT appear in the article's numbers. Every reported $\bar\phi \sim 0.486$ is variant B (raw binary-error Pearson); $n_{\mathrm{eff}} \sim 1.99$ is Kish on B; the $2.06$ ceiling is $1/\bar\phi(\mathrm{B})$; and the $n_{\mathrm{eff}} \sim 1.2$ raw-label figure is variant A. Variant C's value is $-0.0310$ (right next to the $-1/(J-1)$ Kish singularity, Kish $n_{\mathrm{eff}} = 791$, uninterpretable) and it lives only inside \texttt{estimand\_check.py} behind a \texttt{"NEAR SINGULARITY: not interpretable"} guard --- it is never reported. So Prop~1's concern is already satisfied: there is nothing to remove or relabel, because the demeaned-error $\bar\phi$ was never a reported number. Your Prop~1 is exactly why it stays an internal check and not a claim.
